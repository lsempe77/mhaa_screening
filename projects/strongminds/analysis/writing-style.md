# Writing style — ULCM analysis reports (academic register)

The standard for all prose in the ULCM analysis outputs (`.qmd` reports, docs). The aim is a
report that reads as a rigorous evidence-synthesis document written by a methodologist, not
as generated text. Follow this when writing narrative.

## Register
- Formal, precise, impersonal-scientific. A measured first-person plural ("we screened…",
  "we computed…") is acceptable per 3ie convention; promotional or conversational voice is not.
- Report, don't sell. No adjectives of praise for the work ("powerful", "comprehensive",
  "seamless", "state-of-the-art", "novel") unless they are a defensible technical claim.

## Claims and numbers
- Every quantitative statement carries its **N and denominator**, and a **95% CI** where the
  quantity is an estimate. Replace "many / several / substantial / a large share" with the number.
- Consistent rounding; thousands separators; percentages always with their base.
- **Distinguish description from inference.** This work is a descriptive evidence map; do not
  imply causation, pooled effect, or certainty the data do not support. Where a value is
  machine-extracted and not yet human-verified, say so once, plainly, and move on.

## Banned constructions (LLM tells)
Do not use: "it is worth noting", "it is important to note", "importantly", "notably" (as
filler), "delve", "leverage", "robust" (as filler), "landscape", "tapestry", "realm",
"navigate the complexities", "plays a crucial/pivotal/vital role", "in an era of", "a
testament to", "furthermore/moreover" as padding, rhetorical "not only … but also" or "it's
not just X, it's Y", stacked hedges ("may potentially suggest that"), and empty closers
("further research is needed" without saying which research). Prefer plain verbs and concrete
subjects.

## Structure and flow
- Write in **paragraphs**. Reserve bullet lists and tables for genuinely enumerable content
  (a set of criteria, a results table) — not as a substitute for argument.
- Every figure and table is **referenced and interpreted in the text**; none stands alone.
- Vary sentence length. Limit em-dashes. No rhetorical questions. Sentence-case, informative
  headings.

## Terminology and methods
- Define each acronym at first use (RQ, EGM, CCA, AMSTAR-2, ROB, GRADE), then use consistently.
  Fixed nouns: "the corpus", "the evidence base", "the include set".
- Name standard methods where used, with their source: AMSTAR-2 (Shea et al. 2017),
  Corrected Covered Area (Pieper et al. 2014), evidence and gap maps (3ie), GRADE, PRISMA.

## Caveats
- Integrate limitations specifically and in proportion; do not append a performative
  disclaimer paragraph. State what the analysis can and cannot support.
